# Antennas — multiband passive radar (TV + 4G + 5G)

Generated by `antenna_design.py` (closed-form design equations and array-factor math; all numbers are in
`design.json`). There is no EM simulation behind these numbers: **every element must be checked on a VNA and
trimmed** (see *Tuning*). The pattern figures use an infinite-reflector model, so they show the front
half-space only.

## Polarization: +45° slant everywhere

The DVB-T polarization at the site is unknown, so every element and both LPDAs are tilted +45°:
- **4G/5G:** base stations transmit on ±45° cross-polar ports, so a +45° antenna matches one port fully.
- **DVB-T:** a +45° antenna receives either horizontal or vertical TV with a 3 dB loss. That costs about 16% of TV detection range.

If you later learn the TV polarization, you can rotate the UHF elements' stems by 45° to match it. The reflector and pitch stay the same.

## Surveillance element: slant dipole with built-in balun over a flanged reflector

```
     front-face arm (RF) ◄──────┐┌──────► back-face arm (GND)       rotated +45° in the reflector plane
                                ││  ← stem: front strip widens 2.81 → 4 mm, back ground narrows 12 → 4 mm
                                ││     (microstrip-to-parallel-strip taper over the stem height = the balun)
   ════════════════════════════╡╞════════════════════  reflector, slot 16.4 × 2 mm, notches on the stem mark it
                                └┘ edge-mount SMA, behind the reflector
```

- **Board:** FR-4, 1.6 mm, 2 layers, standing at right angles to the reflector. One T-shaped PCB per element.
- **Type:** a double-sided printed dipole, so no vias are needed in the RF path.
- **Front face (net RF):** a 50 Ω microstrip, 2.81 mm wide by OpenBoard's field solver, runs from the SMA up the stem. It widens to a 4 mm parallel strip and becomes the left arm.
- **Back face (net GND):** the microstrip's 12 mm ground narrows to 4 mm along the stem and becomes the right arm.
- **Balun:** the taper runs over the stem height above the reflector, about λ/4. That taper is the balun.

The 54% UHF band is the hard case. The bowtie is sized to resonate at 0.9 × the band centre (559 MHz), so the bottom of the TV band isn't far below resonance. The expected cost is about 1.2 dB of mismatch loss at 470 MHz.

## Panel (reflector) and array

- **Element positions:** 4 elements on one reflector, at the pitch below (λ/2 at the top of each band).
- **Reflector length:** the array length plus 0.75 × the reflector width.
- **Flanges:** folded back along both long edges. With the reflector width of ≥ 0.8 λ, they are what gives the ≥ 20 dB front-to-back that the direct-signal budget needs (DESIGN.md §5).

**Coverage:** the usable sector per panel is **±50°** (100°). Beyond that, sidelobes rise and scan loss passes 3 dB. For 360°, use **4 panels per band**, or several sites. At the bottom of the UHF band (d = 0.28 λ), the beam widens to 48°, so TV direction finding there is coarser. Bistatic-range multilateration across several transmitters (DESIGN.md §7) covers for it.

- **LPDA-H:** 26.7, 23.5, 20.6, 18.2, 16.0, 14.1, 12.4, 10.9, 9.6, 8.4, 7.4

How they're built:
- **LPDA-U:**
  - two 12 mm square aluminium booms, 8 mm apart;
  - 4 mm rod half-elements alternating between the booms;
  - semi-rigid coax run inside one boom (an "infinite balun"), fed at the short end, with the booms shorted at the long end.
- **LPDA-H:**
  - the same structure printed on FR-4: a 3 mm boom on each face (RF in front, GND behind), with 1.5 mm half-dipoles;
  - each dipole has one half on the front face and one on the back, swapping sides from one dipole to the next (the feed transposition);
  - fed by an edge-mount SMA at the short end. Run the cable back along the boom's centre line, where the dipoles' fields cancel.

**Mounting:** tilt each reference +45° to match the panels, aim it at its transmitter, and mount it on the far side of the mast from the surveillance panels.

**Gain check:** DESIGN.md §5 assumed a 12 dBi reference Yagi, giving a reference SNR of +70.8 dB. With 7.5 dBi it's about +66 dB. That's still well above the requirement of +53.8 dB, which is the direct-signal level in the surveillance channel plus 10 dB. The 4G/5G cells are usually much closer than the TV tower, so their margin is larger.

## Cables and calibration

Board A2's noise source calibrates everything from its input switch onward. **The antenna cables are outside that calibration.**

- **Cables:** use a phase-matched set for CH1–CH4 (±2° at the top of the band, e.g. LMR-240 or RG-402 cut as one batch), of equal length.
- **Residual cable error:** remove it with a one-time far-field calibration. The direct signal from the transmitter, whose bearing is known from the map, is a free calibration source; repeat it after any cable change.
- **Mutual coupling:** about −15 dB between neighbouring elements at λ/2. The same far-field calibration (a coupling matrix measured at a few known bearings) removes it for DOA.

## Tuning (VNA)

1. **Resonance:** trim both arm tips equally (copper and board together) until S11 has its minimum at the resonance in the table. If the resonance comes out low, that's the fix. If it's high, change `dipole_length_mm` in `antenna_design.py` and regenerate.
2. **Match:** the balun has no stub to trim. If S11 is poor across the band, change the 4 mm parallel-strip width (`W_PS` in `antenna_pcbs.py`). Wider lowers the feed impedance. Target VSWR < 2; for UHF, < 3 at 470 MHz is expected.
3. **Front-to-back:** check against the transmitter with the panel facing away. If it's below 20 dB, raise the flanges, or add an RF fence or terrain shielding (DESIGN.md §6.1).
4. **LPDAs:** if the low end is weak, trim the shortest dipoles last. S11 at the low edge is set by the longest dipole.
5. **Radome:** do steps 1–2 with the radome cover fitted, since it pulls resonance down slightly. See `RADOME.md`.

## Radomes

All four surveillance panels share one radome. The UHF panel is on top, the three 4G/5G panels sit in a row below, and there is one aluminium tray and one moulded GRP cover: 3 mm over UHF, 2 mm over the 4G/5G window, with a rib between. Separate per-panel radomes remain an alternative. The LPDA-H gets an ASA paddle radome. Dry loss is under 0.45 dB in every band out to ±50°, and a water film costs up to 2.9 dB at n78. Sizes, losses, wind loads and renders are in `RADOME.md`.

## Limits of this design

- **No EM simulation:** you chose formulas only. Gain, beamwidth and sidelobes come from a thin-dipole, infinite-reflector model. Real panels will have about 1 dB less gain at band edges, and their front-to-back depends on the flange details.
- **Matching estimates** come from published results for printed dipoles of this kind, not from calculation. Plan one test build per band before ordering a full set.
- **Sheet metal is drawing-only:** the reflectors, flanges and LPDA-U are given as dimensions, not CAD files.
